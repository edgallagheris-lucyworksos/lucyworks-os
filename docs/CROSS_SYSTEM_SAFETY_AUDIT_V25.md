# LucyWorks v25 cross-system safety audit

This audit separates **clinical decisions**, **operational controls**, **staff concerns**, **safety incidents**, **complaints** and **known design hazards**. A single event may cross several domains, but each consequence must remain visible to the correct people without duplicating ownership or exposing restricted information.

## Control model

`signal → immediate protection → named ownership → accountable action → escalation → independent verification → closure review → recurrence control`

LucyWorks may hold, alert, assign, restrict, reconcile and evidence. It must not diagnose, prescribe, change doses, administer medication, invent consent, acknowledge a result, admit, discharge or make a clinical phase decision.

| Section | Weakness found | Failure that may arise | v25 tightening | Remaining deployment dependency |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Identity and attribution | Older routes accept actor names and roles in JSON payloads. | A browser can appear to create, approve or acknowledge a record as another person. | Verified-attribution middleware overwrites legacy actor fields with the signed-in identity and auth source. | Real OIDC group mapping and subject-to-staff mapping. |
| Access and confidentiality | General HR views expose organisation-wide absence, overtime and welfare records; staff concerns had no restricted case model. | Unnecessary disclosure, retaliation risk, line-manager conflict and unsafe informal handling. | HR legacy surface is senior-only. Staff conduct, bullying, harassment, retaliation and grievance records are forced to strict confidentiality with access logging. | Formal HR role, delegated investigator groups and organisation retention policy. |
| Staff welfare and fatigue | Fatigue creates a gate but was not linked to safe cover, service impact or a confidential investigation record. | An exhausted person remains assigned; the issue is seen as HR administration rather than patient risk. | Medium/high fatigue creates a restricted staff-welfare safety record, accountable owner, work item, safe-cover impact and optional safety hold. | Working-time policy, rota feeds and subject-bound employee self-service. |
| Competence and scope of practice | Competence records exist separately from live work and incident review. | Staff are allocated outside current competence or supervision limits. | Safety records can link staff, competency, shift, room and episode; conflicted or unsuitable owners are rejected. | Real competency catalogue, registration verification and assignment-time enforcement. |
| Absence and capacity | Absence is recorded but may not automatically restrict services or reassign every affected case. | Rooms remain shown as available, referrals continue and patients become unowned. | Safety records carry sanitised operational impact and create named work; board indicators expose service risk without HR detail. | Real service-capacity rules, rota integration and automatic affected-case enumeration. |
| Conduct, bullying and retaliation | No canonical confidential workflow, independent investigator or manager-bypass route. | Concerns disappear into email, are seen by the accused manager, or are closed without protection. | Strict records, protected reporter visibility, conflict subjects, independent owner, access log, ownership rejection/escalation and independent closure. | HR policy, whistleblowing route, union/companion rules and legal retention configuration. |
| Patient deterioration and blockers | Patient-care state and schedule blockers could exist without a canonical incident, closure gate or independent verification. | Red states are visible but unowned, verbally resolved or cleared without proof. | Authenticated patient blockers automatically create patient-safety records, hold requests, links, named clinical ownership and closure gates. | Device/lab feeds, observation thresholds and hospital escalation policy. |
| Referral and triage | Referral decisions, identity review and operational acceptance are in different modules. | Duplicate patient, incomplete history, wrong service or acceptance without capacity. | Safety records can cross-link referral, identity review, patient episode, capacity and decision evidence under one owner. | Referring-practice integration and local acceptance criteria. |
| Consent | Consent versions exist but generic state routes may also carry consent labels. | A workflow label is mistaken for legally sufficient consent evidence. | Safety layer treats missing/withdrawn consent as a blocker; closure cannot substitute for the domain consent record. | Final retirement of generic consent state writes and approved consent templates. |
| Estimates, insurance and financial communication | Estimate and insurance states can be operationally inconsistent with consent and owner communication. | Procedure proceeds beyond authority or owner is not informed of material change. | Mixed patient/operations records can link estimate, consent, communication and procedure blockers without exposing unrelated financial detail. | Practice-specific approval ceilings and insurer integrations. |
| Medication and pharmacy | Medication orders, administration, catalogue and pharmacy readiness are separate control surfaces. | Wrong product, dose, route, stock, legal status or discharge medication gap. | Safety records link medication evidence and corrective work but cannot create orders, doses or administrations. Red closure requires independently verified actions. | Validated formulary, VMD data licence, pharmacy interfaces and controlled-drug SOP. |
| Diagnostics and critical results | Result acknowledgement existed separately from broader incident action and verification. | Result is acknowledged without adequate action, routed to the wrong person or lost at handover. | Critical results create patient-safety records and clinical-review actions. Acknowledgement records action but the incident remains open until independent verification. | Lab/imaging vendor identity, escalation deadlines and on-call routing. |
| Handover | Payload identities were trusted and a pending/rejected handover was not a canonical incident. | Responsibility is assumed to have transferred when no recipient accepted it. | Authenticated sender/recipient decisions, risk-bearing handover safety record, explicit pending state and escalation on rejection. | Staff-directory subject identifiers and deadline escalation scheduler. |
| Scheduling, rooms and equipment | Operational conflict, room state, equipment state and patient workflow can diverge. | A case appears ready in one screen and blocked in another. | Safety record links all source entities and publishes one sanitised board indicator. | Real equipment telemetry and one organisation/premises context. |
| Discharge and owner communication | Discharge readiness, medication, results, owner updates and referring-vet reports can close independently. | Patient leaves with missing medicine, unreviewed result or incomplete instructions. | Safety actions and closure gate require all linked corrective work to be completed and verified; the safety layer cannot discharge. | Final consolidated discharge command and client communication integration. |
| Safeguarding and complaints | Safeguarding, complaint, conduct and patient-safety facts may overlap but need different visibility and authorities. | Staff allegations appear in patient records, or patient risk is hidden inside HR confidentiality. | Mixed-domain record retains restricted detail while board indicator exposes only patient/operational protection. Links have their own visibility. | Named safeguarding lead, complaint policy and external-reporting rules. |
| Automation and AI | Multiple automation layers can create work but must not silently adapt from incident outcomes. | AI appears to make a clinical decision or uses confidential staff allegations for optimisation. | Safety workflow records human decisions, forbids clinical acts and separates restricted records from public indicators. | Model register, DPIA, data-use controls and explicit no-training policy enforcement. |
| Integrations and data integrity | Vendor feeds may duplicate, mismatch or arrive late. | Wrong patient, stale result, missed acknowledgement or duplicate work. | Data-integrity records can hold operations, link canonical source versions and require reconciliation/verification. | Vendor sandboxes, message contracts, replay and failover tests. |
| Downtime and recovery | A failure may be recorded in deployment readiness but not linked to affected patients or staff workload. | Manual fallback starts without named reconciliation or patient follow-up. | Operational-failure records link downtime, affected episodes, workload, recovery work and closure verification. | Tested downtime forms, local recovery objectives and contact tree. |
| Incident investigation and closure | Existing hazards describe design risk, not real-event investigation. No single closure standard existed. | Red incidents are marked resolved after one note, with no root cause or recurrence control. | Red/critical closure requires actions, independent verification, root cause, recurrence controls and independent closure approval. | Organisation investigation methodology and regulator/insurer reporting configuration. |
| Reporting and board visibility | Parallel HR, control-plane, patient and safety boards can show conflicting statuses. | Staff do not know where to look or disclose confidential details to make risk visible. | One Safety Control surface plus a sanitised board-indicator endpoint; detailed restricted records remain role-gated. | Embed indicators into Patient Command and Hospital Today after device and access UAT. |

## Crossovers that must create one record, not several competing incidents

1. **Fatigued clinician + delayed procedure + deteriorating patient** — staff details restricted; patient risk visible; operational service restriction visible; one accountable safety record with several links and owners.
2. **Bullying allegation + unsafe staffing decision** — allegation strict; immediate rota protection visible without allegation text; conflicted manager excluded.
3. **Critical result + rejected handover** — one patient-safety record links the result, handover, acknowledgement action and escalation.
4. **Missing consent + estimate increase + emergency treatment** — patient and client-information controls linked; clinical decision remains with authorised veterinary staff.
5. **Vendor identity mismatch + medication proposal** — data-integrity hold prevents downstream reliance; no medication action is committed by automation.
6. **Downtime + manual records + later import** — operational-failure record owns reconciliation, duplicate checks and unresolved patient follow-up.

## Closure standard

A record is not closed because the immediate pressure has passed. Closure requires:

- no open safety actions;
- independent verification where required;
- no conflicted closer;
- root cause and recurrence controls for red/critical records;
- independent closure approval;
- retained evidence of who did what, when and why;
- reopening when new evidence or recurrence appears.
